# The Huijian Murder Case (English)

Huijian was the online name of Wu Kai. Wu Kai was originally a website planner in Shenzhen. In 2012, Wu Kai exposed scandals involving a real estate mogul on Weibo and began to be known to the public; later he opened a WeChat public account exposing the realities of telecom fraud in Myanmar and Cambodia, and had hundreds of thousands of followers.

In August 2024, Wu Kai publicly accused Liu Ren, president of the Anhui Chamber of Commerce in Cambodia, saying that he engaged in violent loan sharking at casinos, caused the death of a renovation business owner, extorted protection fees from businesses, operated fraud compounds, and engaged in armed cross-border wrongdoing; this was the first time Liu Ren had been publicly accused by name. Liu Ren's side denied what he said.

In mid-October of the same year, a person using the online name "Shaoxiao" (also known as "Tai Ge", meaning "Major") offered a reward of 300,000 yuan on Telegram to have Wu Kai abducted. Zhao Bing, from Minquan in Henan, born in December 1998, took up the offer; the two later communicated through the encrypted app Yeechat. Zhao Bing recruited accomplices and raised the price to 1 million yuan. The instructions were twofold: first, find out who had directed Wu Kai's posts, and second, delete his posts.

On November 10, men posing as police seized Wu Kai in Huizhou, Guangdong; on the same day, Zhao Bing's accomplice Chen Jianhua received the agreed payment in cash from a person sent by Shaoxiao. The group took Wu Kai through Heyuan in Guangdong to Ganzhou in Jiangxi; on the way they beat and interrogated him, deleted his posts, and handed his phone to a person designated by Shaoxiao.

The next day, Zhao Bing kicked Wu Kai unconscious in the hills of Dingnan in Jiangxi and abandoned him after dark. On November 13, villagers found his body.

After Wu Kai's death, rumors spread that he had been arrested for extortion; his friend Zhang Yangfei (a pseudonym) said that because of this rumor, he believed Wu Kai was in prison, and only when reporters asked him did he learn that Wu Kai had been killed. A friend of many years, Lao An (a pseudonym), said that a few days after Wu Kai's death, someone transferred 11,000 yuan to his Alipay account, screenshots of the transfer circulated, and stories were then fabricated that he was involved in human trafficking, extortion, and tax evasion and had been taken away by the police; Lao An also said that Wu Kai's parents had died and he had no close relatives to speak for him, so after his disappearance, no one spoke out for him.

The Huizhou Intermediate People's Court found that Zhao Bing's assault on Wu Kai during his detention caused his death. On December 29, 2025, the court sentenced Zhao Bing to death with a two-year reprieve for intentional injury, and deprived him of political rights for life; the other thirteen defendants were sentenced for intentional injury, unlawful detention, harboring offenders, and other offenses; fourteen people in total were convicted in the case. Zhao Bing paid 500,000 yuan in compensation to Wu Kai's family through his mother; in December of the same year, Wu Kai's son Wu Zhengjie signed a letter of forgiveness, forgiving all fourteen; Wu Zhengjie told The Beijing News that before signing, two people connected with the defendants' families came to his home looking for his father, that he only learned who they were after calling the police, and that he signed to avoid further trouble.

On August 12, 2026, the Guangdong High People's Court upheld the original verdicts, saying that abducting and assaulting an anti-fraud campaigner seriously harmed society, and that compensation and forgiveness were not enough to reduce the sentences. On June 17 of the same year, Xinhua, citing the Ministry of Public Security, said that Liu Ren had been brought back from Cambodia, describing him as a key member of Chen Zhi's criminal group, involved in online gambling and fraud compounds, and suspected of unlawful detention and intentional injury; however, that announcement did not link Liu Ren to Wu Kai's killing, nor did it link Liu Ren to Shaoxiao. Wu Kai's relatives said that, to their knowledge, Shaoxiao remained abroad and had not been arrested.

All of the above is retold from publicly reported news and court rulings; accusations and rumors are attributed to their sources, and what has not been confirmed is not presented as established fact.
